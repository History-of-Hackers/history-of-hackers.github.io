Whitfield Diffie and Martin Hellman

Person · 1976-Present

Whitfield Diffie and Martin Hellman co-authored the 1976 paper "New Directions in Cryptography," introducing public-key cryptography and the Diffie-Hellman key exchange, and jointly received the 2015 ACM A.M. Turing Award for this work.

Ken Thompson

Person · 1969-Present

Ken Thompson is an American computer scientist who co-created the Unix operating system with Dennis Ritchie at Bell Labs, later co-designed the Go programming language at Google, and authored the seminal 1984 Turing Award lecture "Reflections on Trusting Trust," which exposed how a compiler could be backdoored to insert undetectable trojans into software.

Loïc Duflot

Person · 2006-2011 (documented research period)

French security researcher employed by France's DCSSI (later ANSSI) who, from 2006 onward, showed that x86 System Management Mode, ACPI, and PC chipset/firmware features could be abused to bypass operating-system and hypervisor security guarantees, first presenting this work publicly at CanSecWest 2006.

Philip Zimmermann

Person · 1991-Present

Philip R. Zimmermann is the American computer scientist who created Pretty Good Privacy (PGP) in 1991, the first widely used public-key email encryption program, and who was the target of a three-year US federal criminal investigation for allegedly exporting munitions without a license after PGP spread internationally.

Ron Rivest, Adi Shamir, and Leonard Adleman

Person · 1977-Present

MIT researchers Ron Rivest, Adi Shamir, and Leonard Adleman publicly described the RSA public-key cryptosystem in 1977-1978, a foundational algorithm for secure communication and digital signatures that earned them the 2002 ACM Turing Award.

Steve Wozniak

Person · 1970s-Present

Steve Wozniak is an electronics engineer who, before co-founding Apple Computer with Steve Jobs, built and sold illegal "blue box" devices for phone phreaking in the early 1970s -- an episode both men later credited as a formative step toward Apple's creation.
