Nadia Heninger

Heninger joins mathematical cryptanalysis to internet-scale measurement, showing how subtle implementation choices can weaken cryptography in systems people actually use.

Nadia Heninger is a professor in the University of California, San Diego Department of Computer Science and Engineering. Her research centers on cryptography and security, especially mathematical cryptanalysis applied to real systems.

From theory to deployed systems

Her work uses computational number theory, coding theory, lattice methods, and network measurement to examine public-key cryptography as deployed. A secure primitive can still fail when keys, parameters, or implementations are weak.

Academic contribution

After doctoral work at Princeton and positions at Microsoft Research, UC San Diego, and the University of Pennsylvania, Heninger returned to UC San Diego. Her research helped make empirical assessment of deployed cryptography central to modern security research.
