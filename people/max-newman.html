Max Newman

Newman helped connect mathematical cryptanalysis to automated computation. The Newmanry’s work against Lorenz traffic is a key chapter in the co-evolution of security analysis and programmable electronic computing.

Max Newman was a British mathematician and cryptanalyst. At Bletchley Park he led the section later called the Newmanry, which developed mechanised methods for attacking Lorenz-encrypted German messages.

Computing in cryptanalysis

Newman’s group defined a demanding cryptanalytic problem and worked with engineers including Tommy Flowers to automate parts of its solution. The resulting Colossus machines were used operationally during the war.

In memoriam

Newman died in 1984. His academic and wartime work helped shape the early history of computing.
