Howard H. Aiken

Aiken’s Mark I project brought together academic, military, and industrial resources around automatic computation. It supplied an important working environment for early programmers including Grace Hopper.

Howard H. Aiken proposed an automatic sequence-controlled calculator while at Harvard. IBM built the resulting Harvard Mark I, which was placed in operation during the Second World War.

A working computing project

The Mark I was electromechanical and not a stored-program computer, but it demonstrated the organizational and engineering scale of automatic calculation. Its history also includes the work of many operators and programmers.

In memoriam

Aiken died in 1973. The Mark I remains a prominent landmark in the history of large-scale computing.
