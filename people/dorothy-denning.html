Dorothy E. Denning

Denning's research helped articulate early models for detecting computer intrusions and for controlling information flow. Her academic and public-policy work makes her an important figure in the development of computer-security research.

Dorothy E. Denning is an American computer-security researcher whose work has addressed cryptography, access control, information warfare, and intrusion detection. She held research and teaching positions at Purdue, SRI International, Digital Equipment Corporation, Georgetown University, and the Naval Postgraduate School.

Intrusion detection

Her 1987 article An Intrusion-Detection Model presented a model for identifying unauthorized activity from audit data. The work is commonly cited in the research history of intrusion-detection systems.

Security research and public discussion

Denning has also written about cryptography policy, cyber conflict, and the interaction between technical security and society. The record of her work illustrates how computer-security research combines formal models, system observation, and public-policy questions.

Timeline

  • 1975Completes a Ph.D. in computer science at Purdue University.
  • 1983Leaves Purdue for a position at SRI International.
