Kevin Poulsen

Kevin Poulsen, known by the handle 'Dark Dante,' was a phone phreaker and hacker who rigged a 1990 radio-station phone contest to win a Porsche and later reinvented himself as an award-winning Wired News journalist who helped uncover Chelsea Manning as the source of the WikiLeaks military and diplomatic leaks.

Kevin Lee Poulsen was born on November 30, 1965, in Pasadena, California. Under the handle 'Dark Dante,' he became one of the more notorious phone phreakers and computer hackers of the 1980s.

The KIIS-FM Porsche Hack

Poulsen's most famous exploit came on June 1, 1990, when he took over all of the telephone lines belonging to Los Angeles radio station KIIS-FM. By seizing control of the station's phone system, he guaranteed that he would be the contest's 102nd caller, the position needed to win the on-air prize: a Porsche 944 S2.

Fugitive, Arrest, and Conviction

As federal investigators closed in on his hacking activities, Poulsen went underground as a fugitive from the FBI. He was arrested in April 1991. In June 1994, Poulsen pleaded guilty to seven counts of conspiracy, fraud, and wiretapping, and was sentenced to 51 months in federal prison, along with a three-year post-release ban on using computers and the internet. He was released in 1999.

Reinvention as a Journalist

After his release, Poulsen pivoted from hacking to writing about it, joining the security news outlet SecurityFocus around 2000. In June 2005 he moved to Wired News as a senior editor, where he ran a security-focused blog later renamed 'Threat Level.'

The Chelsea Manning/Adrian Lamo Story

In May 2010, U.S. Army intelligence analyst Chelsea Manning confided by chat to former hacker Adrian Lamo that she had leaked classified military and diplomatic material to WikiLeaks. Lamo passed the story to Poulsen under embargo. Poulsen, together with Wired colleague Kim Zetter, broke the story on June 6, 2010, publishing a portion of the Lamo-Manning chat logs; Wired released the remaining logs in July 2011.

Legacy

Poulsen went on to co-create SecureDrop (originally called DeadDrop, then Strongbox) with programmer-activist Aaron Swartz and developer James Dolan, an open-source platform that lets journalists receive material from anonymous sources securely. He also authored the 2011 book 'Kingpin,' about cybercrime. Poulsen is frequently cited as an example of a hacker whose criminal past led into a substantial career in investigative technology journalism.
