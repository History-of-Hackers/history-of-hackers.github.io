Grace Murray Hopper

Hopper made programming languages more practical and more accessible to organizations. Her compiler work is foundational to software development, whose quality and maintainability remain prerequisites for secure systems.

Grace Murray Hopper worked on the Harvard Mark I during the Second World War and later helped create early compiler systems. She was a major contributor to the development and promotion of COBOL.

From instructions to languages

Hopper advocated languages that let people state business and technical operations more directly. Her work helped define how organizations would build and operate software for decades.

In memoriam

Hopper died in 1992. Her public service and technical work are commemorated across computing and the U.S. Navy.
