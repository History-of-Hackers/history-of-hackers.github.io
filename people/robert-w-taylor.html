Robert W. Taylor

Taylor's influence was organizational as well as technical: he assembled and funded research groups that produced foundational networked-computing systems. This is relevant to hacker and security history because it situates later network practice in its institutional and technical origins.

Robert William Taylor was an American computer-science research manager and leader. He worked at ARPA during the formative period of interactive-computing and networking research.

Networks and personal computing

The Computer History Museum credits Taylor with initiating ARPANET and with sponsoring interactive-computing research. At Xerox PARC, he founded the Computer Science Laboratory, where teams worked on Ethernet, the Alto, and networked personal-computing systems.

In memoriam

Taylor retired in 1996 and died in 2017. His record is a reminder that major computing systems are built by communities of engineers and researchers, with program leadership helping sustain their work.

Timeline

  • 1969Leaves ARPA after the initial ARPANET program period.

  • 2017-04-13Dies in Woodside, California.
