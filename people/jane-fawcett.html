Jane Fawcett

Fawcett’s story connects wartime communications intelligence with the large, often uncredited workforce that made cryptanalytic output usable to decision-makers.

Jane Fawcett joined Bletchley Park as a teenager during the Second World War. In Hut 6 she worked with decrypted German messages, an operational role within the larger Allied intelligence process.

Historical significance

Published accounts connect Fawcett with the handling of a message that helped identify the location of the battleship Bismarck. The broader intelligence outcome relied on many stages of interception, decryption, analysis and naval action; this page therefore credits the collective system as well as her documented service.

In memoriam

Fawcett died in 2016. Her legacy also includes decades of work in architectural conservation.
