John Backus

Backus helped make high-level languages practical at scale. Compilers and language design remain security-relevant because they shape how developers express, analyze, and constrain program behavior.

John Backus led the IBM project that produced FORTRAN, one of the first widely used high-level programming languages. He later proposed a functional programming approach intended to reduce some complexity of conventional programming.

Language as infrastructure

FORTRAN’s success established the compiler as a central component of computing. Subsequent language and compiler work has been essential to dependable systems and software assurance.

In memoriam

Backus died in 2007. His work remains a landmark in the history of programming languages.
