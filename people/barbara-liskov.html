Barbara Liskov

Liskov’s work provides foundations for making complex software more reliable: abstraction, distributed programming and fault-tolerant replication are central ideas in modern secure systems engineering.

Barbara Liskov is an MIT computer scientist whose research spans programming methodology, languages and distributed systems. Her projects include CLU, Argus and later work on Byzantine-fault-tolerant storage.

Dependable systems

Her research helped establish ways to reason about data abstraction and behavior in programs, and it advanced mechanisms for distributed systems that continue functioning despite faults or malicious participants. These ideas have enduring relevance to security engineering, though they are not a substitute for complete security design.

Recognition

Liskov received the ACM A.M. Turing Award for practical and theoretical contributions to programming-language and system design.
