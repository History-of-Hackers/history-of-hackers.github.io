Solomon Kullback

Kullback brought rigorous statistical methods to cryptanalysis and later co-developed the Kullback–Leibler divergence, an enduring tool for measuring differences between probability distributions.

Solomon Kullback was an American mathematician, cryptanalyst, and educator. He joined the U.S. Army Signal Intelligence Service in 1930 and worked in cryptanalysis and communications security through the Second World War and the early National Security Agency period.

Statistics and cryptology

Kullback’s published work connected mathematical statistics to the practical evaluation and solution of cipher systems. With Richard Leibler, he developed the divergence that bears their names, now widely used in information theory, statistics, and machine learning.

In memoriam

Kullback died in 1994. His work remains a durable bridge between cryptologic practice and mathematical research.
