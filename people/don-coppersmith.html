Don Coppersmith

Coppersmith's work spans the mathematics beneath practical cryptography and the analysis used to find when a system's parameters leave it vulnerable. His results remain reference points in RSA cryptanalysis and computer algebra.

Research at IBM

Don Coppersmith is a mathematician and cryptographer whose work at IBM Research has covered DES, discrete-logarithm algorithms and attacks on public-key systems. IBM's publication archive describes his work on both DES and the Diffie–Hellman setting.

Modular equations and cryptanalysis

Coppersmith is especially known in cryptography for methods for finding small roots of modular polynomial equations. Such techniques are useful when an implementation or protocol leaks enough structure to turn a hard number-theory problem into an attack. His research is a reminder that the security of a cryptosystem depends on parameter choices and implementation context, not only on its headline algorithm.

  • 1977Joins IBM's Thomas J. Watson Research Center.
