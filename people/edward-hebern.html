Edward Hugh Hebern

Hebern’s rotor-machine work helped establish the technological lineage that later included Enigma and other electromechanical cipher systems. It illustrates how a security design can be historically important even when its deployment is limited.

Edward Hugh Hebern was an American inventor who developed early rotor-based cipher machinery in the 1910s and 1920s. His electrical coding machines used rotors to implement changing substitutions for encrypted text.

Technical legacy

Smithsonian collection material identifies Hebern as the designer of a rotor for a cipher machine. Later rotor systems became a major class of twentieth-century cryptographic equipment, making his work an important part of the prehistory of machine cryptography.

In memoriam

Hebern died in 1952. His early experiments remain part of the record of how electromechanical cryptography developed.

Timeline
