Cynthia Dwork

Differential privacy provides a rigorous framework for reasoning about disclosure risk in statistical analysis. Dwork's work has made privacy a central, mathematically explicit concern in modern data analysis and security research.

Cynthia Dwork is a computer scientist and Harvard professor whose research uses theoretical computer science to study privacy, cryptography, distributed computing, and algorithmic questions. She has also held a distinguished-scientist role at Microsoft.

Differential privacy

Dwork was among the authors of the research that introduced and developed differential privacy: a formal approach to limiting what can be learned about an individual from the result of a computation over a data set.

Research impact

The framework has become a major reference point for privacy-preserving data analysis. Her awards include the Gödel Prize, the Knuth Prize, and the ACM Paris Kanellakis Theory and Practice Award shared with collaborators for contributions to differential privacy.

Timeline

  • 1983Completes a Ph.D. at Cornell University.
  • 2006Coauthors Calibrating Noise to Sensitivity in Private Data Analysis.
  • 2017Receives the Gödel Prize with collaborators for work on differential privacy.
  • 2020Receives the Knuth Prize.
