Agnes Meyer Driscoll

Driscoll combined manual and machine-oriented cryptanalysis at a formative point in U.S. naval cryptology. Her work illustrates the continuity between codebreaking, system evaluation, and the technical security profession.

Agnes Meyer Driscoll was an American cryptanalyst who served the U.S. Navy’s cryptologic organizations from the First World War era through the early Cold War. Her career covered both cipher-machine work and analysis of Japanese naval communications.

Technical legacy

The National Security Agency identifies Driscoll as a leading Navy cryptanalyst who worked against multiple Japanese naval systems and contributed to early machine approaches to cryptography. Her experience made her an important technical continuity figure in an organization whose military personnel frequently rotated.

In memoriam

Driscoll died in 1971. Her career remains a landmark in the history of women in cryptology and technical intelligence.

Timeline

  • 1889Born in Ohio, United States.
  • 1918Enlists in the U.S. Navy and is assigned to cryptologic work.
  • 1923Works as technical adviser to the Hebern Electric Code Company.
  • 1924Returns to Navy cryptologic work.
  • 1940Makes critical inroads into the Japanese JN-25 system, according to NSA history.
  • 1971Dies; later recognized in the NSA/CSS Hall of Honor.
