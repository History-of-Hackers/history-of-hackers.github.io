Joseph O. Mauborgne

Mauborgne’s work joined communications engineering with cryptology. His one-time-pad work remains essential to the history of perfect secrecy and secure key use.

Joseph Oswald Mauborgne was a U.S. Army Signal Corps officer, inventor, and cryptologist. His career linked radio communications, cipher equipment, and cryptanalytic work.

One-time-pad work

In 1919 Mauborgne developed a one-time-pad system with randomly selected key material, extending Gilbert Vernam’s teleprinter-cipher idea. When correctly used with truly random nonrepeating key material, the one-time pad is the classic example of perfect secrecy.

Operational cryptology

Mauborgne also worked on the M-94 cylinder cipher device, which the U.S. Army used for decades. His career illustrates the practical side of making and operating secure communications systems.

In memoriam

Mauborgne died in 1971. The National Security Agency later inducted him into its Cryptologic Hall of Honor.
