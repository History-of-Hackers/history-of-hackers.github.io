Adi Shamir

Shamir’s research made durable contributions to public-key cryptography and to the language and practice of modern cryptanalysis.

Adi Shamir is a cryptographer whose work has shaped modern information security. With Ron Rivest and Leonard Adleman, he co-invented RSA, a public-key cryptosystem that became one of the best-known tools in digital security.

Foundations

Shamir also introduced a widely used secret-sharing method and has contributed influential ideas in cryptanalysis. His work matters to hacker culture and security history because it made the assumptions behind secure communication concrete, testable, and contestable.

Recognition

The ACM recognized the RSA inventors with the Turing Award in 2002, reflecting the cryptosystem’s enduring significance.

Timeline

  • 1977Co-invents the RSA public-key cryptosystem with Ron Rivest and Leonard Adleman.
  • 1979Introduces the secret-sharing scheme now known as Shamir secret sharing.
  • 2002Receives the ACM Turing Award with Rivest and Adleman for RSA.
  • PresentContinues scholarly work in cryptography and related fields.
