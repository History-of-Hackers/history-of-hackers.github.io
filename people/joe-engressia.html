Joe Engressia

Engressia was among the earliest people to demonstrate publicly that the telephone network's in-band signaling could be manipulated by an ordinary user, a finding that helped define phone phreaking as a distinct practice. His case illustrates an early and influential form of exploratory hacking: control signals shared the same audio channel as speech, so anyone able to reproduce those signals could direct the switching equipment. Media coverage of his 1968 college suspension and 1971 conviction, together with Rosenbaum's Esquire piece, brought the phreak underground to a mass audience and connected participants who had previously worked in isolation. That publicity is widely credited with influencing later technologists, including Steve Wozniak and Steve Jobs, whose blue box venture preceded the founding of Apple.

Early life

Josef Carl Engressia Jr. was born on 25 May 1949 in Richmond, Virginia, to Joe Sr. and Esther Engressia. He was blind from birth, as was his younger sister Toni. The family moved frequently because of his father's work as a school photographer, and Engressia spent his childhood in Virginia, Massachusetts and several Florida cities. He had absolute pitch and an unusual sensitivity to sound and texture, and he became fascinated by the telephone at around the age of four.

Discovery of the 2600 Hz tone

As a small child Engressia learned to place calls by rapidly tapping the telephone's hookswitch, in effect pulse-dialing by hand. A few years later, while idly whistling during a long-distance call, he found that certain pitches caused the call to cut off. He had unknowingly reproduced the 2600 Hz supervisory tone that the Bell System used to mark a long-distance trunk as idle. Because that signaling travelled in the same audio band as the conversation, a caller who could generate the tone accurately could seize a trunk and then direct it elsewhere. Accounts of his age at the discovery vary slightly; The New York Times placed it in 1957, and other accounts give his age as about seven.

The Whistler

By the late 1960s Engressia was a student at the University of South Florida, where he acquired the nickname "Whistler" for his ability to place free long-distance calls without any equipment. He would dial a toll-free or non-working number to reach a distant switching point, whistle 2600 Hz to release the trunk, and then signal a different destination. In 1968 he was caught making calls for other students at a dollar each after a Canadian operator reported him; he was suspended and fined twenty-five dollars, though he was later reinstated and graduated with a degree in philosophy.

Center of the phreak network

Engressia's notoriety made him a hub for a scattered, largely self-taught community of phone enthusiasts across the United States, many of whom telephoned him to trade discoveries about routing, tandem switches and blue boxes. Ron Rosenbaum's October 1971 Esquire article "Secrets of the Little Blue Box" — which also introduced readers to John Draper, known as Captain Crunch — described him as the catalyst that united otherwise disconnected phreaks, writing that he sat "like a sightless spider in his little apartment receiving messages from every tendril of its web". The article is frequently cited as the piece that inspired Steve Wozniak and Steve Jobs to build and sell blue boxes.

Arrest and telephone work

In 1971 Engressia moved to Memphis, Tennessee, where he was convicted on phone fraud charges; he later said he had deliberately arranged his own arrest in the hope of being offered work by the telephone industry. He was subsequently hired in Millington, Tennessee, in a job cleaning telephones, which he disliked. In 1975 he moved to Denver to help identify faults in Mountain Bell's network. He said he had stopped placing free calls well before the mid-1970s, by which time the phone system's shift away from in-band tone signaling was closing off the technique.

Later life as Joybubbles

Engressia moved to Minneapolis on 12 June 1982, choosing the date because 6-12 matched the city's 612 area code. He lived on Social Security disability payments and occasional part-time work, including serving as an odor panelist for university researchers studying livestock waste. Having been sexually abused as a child at a school for the blind, he announced in May 1988 that he had "reverted to his childhood" and would consider himself five years old permanently, and in 1991 he legally changed his name to Joybubbles to separate himself from the name under which the abuse occurred. He was an ordained minister of his own Church of Eternal Childhood, ran a one-man support organization called "We Won't Grow Up", and continued to operate telephone services, among them a weekly recorded story line called "Stories and Stuff" and, in the early and mid-1980s, the "Zzzzyzzerrific Funline", named so that it would appear last in the telephone directory. He was also a licensed amateur radio operator holding an amateur extra class licence with the call sign WB0RPA.

Death and legacy

Joybubbles died in Minneapolis on 8 August 2007 at the age of 58; the cause was not determined at the time of his obituary. He was survived by his mother Esther and sister Toni. His story has been retold in Phil Lapsley's history "Exploding the Phone", in a 2012 Radiolab segment, and in the BBC Radio 4 documentary "A Call from Joybubbles" first broadcast in March 2017. The 1992 film "Sneakers" featured a blind character named Whistler widely understood to draw on Engressia and Draper. He remains a reference point in accounts of how curiosity-driven exploration of a large technical system preceded and shaped later computer hacker culture.
