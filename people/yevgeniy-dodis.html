Yevgeniy Dodis

Security systems often rely on imperfect randomness and noisy real-world data. Dodis's research develops rigorous ways to reason about those constraints in cryptographic designs.

Yevgeniy Dodis is a professor at New York University's Courant Institute and a member of the NYU Cryptography Group.

Randomness and security

His academic profile identifies interests including leakage-resilient cryptography, random-number generation, cryptography with biometrics and other noisy data, and secure group messaging.

Recognition

Dodis received a Ph.D. from MIT in 2000. His research page records IACR fellowship in 2020 and IACR Test of Time Awards for work on fuzzy extractors and verifiable random functions.

Security context

Randomness, key material, and information leakage are practical security concerns. The theoretical tools developed in this area help engineers identify what guarantees a protocol can realistically provide.
