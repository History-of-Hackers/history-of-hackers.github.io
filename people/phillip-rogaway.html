Phillip Rogaway

Rogaway's research treats cryptography as both a mathematical discipline and a public-interest technology, linking protocol design to the social effects of surveillance and security decisions.

Phillip Rogaway is a cryptographer who taught at the University of California, Davis from 1994 until his retirement in 2024. His research has focused on rigorous, practical solutions for confidentiality, authentication, and privacy.

Applied cryptography

Rogaway co-developed important schemes and analyses for authenticated encryption and public-key encryption. His work illustrates why encryption alone is not enough: protocols also need integrity and carefully stated adversary models.

Ethics and security

He has also written and spoken about the ethical responsibility of cryptographers, especially where technical choices enable mass surveillance or concentrate power.
