John McHugh

McHugh's critique of the 1998 and 1999 DARPA intrusion-detection evaluations made evaluation methodology itself a central security-research question. It remains a frequently cited reminder that detection results depend on realistic data, assumptions, and measurements.

John McHugh is a computer-security researcher whose work includes intrusion detection and the evaluation of detection systems. His scholarship examined not only how systems identify suspicious activity but also how claims about detector performance should be tested.

Testing the tests

In a 2000 ACM Transactions on Information and System Security article, McHugh analyzed the 1998 and 1999 DARPA intrusion-detection evaluations performed by Lincoln Laboratory. The critique focused attention on experimental design and the limits of benchmark-driven conclusions.

Security practice

McHugh also contributed to Carnegie Mellon University's Software Engineering Institute work documenting the state of intrusion-detection technologies. That work placed research questions alongside the realities of deployed security products.
