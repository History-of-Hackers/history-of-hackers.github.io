Neal I. Koblitz

Koblitz’s elliptic-curve proposal opened a major line of practical public-key cryptography, later used widely in secure protocols and constrained computing environments.

Neal I. Koblitz is a mathematician at the University of Washington whose research spans number theory and cryptography. University of Washington material identifies him as a co-inventor of elliptic-curve cryptography.

Cryptographic legacy

In 1985, Koblitz proposed using elliptic curves in public-key cryptography. That work, independently paralleled by Victor Miller, became a central foundation of elliptic-curve cryptography.
