Leonard Adleman

Adleman co-created one of public-key cryptography’s defining systems, a milestone in the development of secure digital communication.

Leonard Adleman is a computer scientist and co-inventor of RSA public-key cryptography. RSA became a canonical example of how mathematical hardness assumptions can support encryption and signatures in open communication systems.

Security foundation

Adleman’s contribution to RSA placed him at a key point in the history of modern computer security. The system’s publication encouraged open study, implementation, analysis, and debate about cryptographic design.

Beyond cryptography

His later work in molecular computation illustrates a broad interest in how computation can be realized and analyzed.

Timeline

  • 1977Co-invents RSA with Ron Rivest and Adi Shamir.
  • 1978Publishes the RSA paper with Rivest and Shamir.
  • 1994Publishes a landmark experiment in DNA computation.
  • 2002Receives the ACM Turing Award with Rivest and Shamir.
