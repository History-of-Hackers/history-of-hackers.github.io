William Tutte

Tutte’s reconstruction of the Lorenz cipher from intercepted traffic is a classic example of extracting a system’s structure from its outputs—the central intellectual move of cryptanalysis and security analysis.

William Tutte was a British mathematician and wartime cryptanalyst at Bletchley Park. Working from intercepted ciphertext, he deduced the logical structure of the German Lorenz SZ40/42 cipher machine, known to British analysts as Tunny.

Lorenz and mechanised analysis

Tutte’s analysis enabled the later mechanisation of attacks on Lorenz traffic. That work formed part of the setting in which Colossus was developed and used for high-speed cryptanalytic processing.

In memoriam

Tutte died in 2002. He is remembered both for cryptanalysis and for major contributions to mathematics.

Timeline
