Manuel Blum

Manuel Blum is included for documented contributions to the technical and formal foundations of computer security and cryptography.

Manuel Blum is a theoretical computer scientist whose work made foundational connections between computational complexity and cryptography. His research includes cryptographic pseudorandomness, protocols for key exchange and coin flipping, and program checking.

Security relevance

Blum’s work supplied rigorous ways to reason about computational limits and adversarial uncertainty. His collaborations on pseudorandom generation and zero-knowledge protocols influenced the theoretical toolkit used in modern cryptography.

Timeline

  • 1964Receives a Ph.D. in mathematics from MIT.
  • 1983Publishes “Coin Flipping by Telephone.”
  • 1984Co-authors work on cryptographically strong pseudorandom sequences.
  • 1995Receives the ACM A.M. Turing Award.
