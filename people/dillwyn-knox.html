Alfred Dillwyn Knox

Knox’s work connects linguistic analysis, manual cryptanalysis, and the institutional development of Bletchley Park. It is a reminder that security research has long required more than one technical discipline.

Alfred Dillwyn “Dilly” Knox was a British classicist and cryptanalyst who worked for the Admiralty’s Room 40 and later the Government Code and Cypher School. During the Second World War he led a Bletchley Park section focused on particular Enigma variants.

Legacy at Bletchley Park

GCHQ historical material describes Knox’s role in the development of an Enigma-focused section at Bletchley Park. His methods and mentoring formed part of the wider cryptanalytic effort that linked human insight, intercepts, and machinery.

In memoriam

Knox died in 1943 while the war was still under way. His contribution is remembered as part of the intellectual foundation of British wartime cryptanalysis.
