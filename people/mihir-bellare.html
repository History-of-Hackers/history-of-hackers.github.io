Mihir Bellare

Bellare's work demonstrates how formal security models can guide deployable primitives, including message authentication and encryption schemes used in Internet-facing systems.

Mihir Bellare is a cryptographer and computer-science professor associated with the University of California San Diego. His research focuses on cryptographic definitions, constructions, and proofs with practical relevance.

From models to deployed primitives

Bellare co-developed HMAC and contributed to OAEP, connecting theoretical analysis with widely used authentication and public-key-encryption building blocks. His work on security models has also shaped the way protocol designers state and evaluate guarantees.

Historical significance

His career is a useful bridge between academic cryptography and systems that protect network traffic and electronic transactions.

Timeline

  • 1991Completes a Ph.D. in computer science at MIT.
  • 1995Joins UC San Diego's computer-science faculty.
  • 1997Co-authors RFC 2104, HMAC: Keyed-Hashing for Message Authentication.
  • 2013Named an ACM Fellow for contributions to provable security methods.
