The Matrix

A cyberpunk science-fiction film in which computer programmer-by-day, hacker-by-night Thomas Anderson ('Neo') discovers that perceived reality is a simulation run by machines, and joins a band of rebel hackers to fight back. It is one of the most influential works in hacker-themed cinema.
This is a fiction/pop-culture entry, not a historical record. Everything on this page describes a fictional work and its characters. These are not real people or documented events — see Hackers in Popular Culture for the sourced history of how film and TV have portrayed hacking, and the rest of the site for real, verified figures.

The Matrix follows Thomas A. Anderson, a programmer who leads a double life as the hacker "Neo," trafficking in illegal software and hacking programs. He is contacted by the hacker Trinity and recruited by Morpheus, leader of a small resistance crew. Morpheus reveals that the world Neo has always known is a simulated construct generated by machines to pacify humanity while harvesting people's bodies for energy, and offers Neo a choice to learn the truth and join the fight to free humanity.

Hacker Culture Details

The film's opening act is built around hacker-culture texture: Neo is introduced monitoring a hacker chatroom, and his double life as a software pirate is signaled by a hollowed-out copy of Jean Baudrillard's Simulacra and Simulation shown on screen. Trinity is introduced by reputation as a hacker legendary enough to have once cracked a heavily secured IRS database. A frequently repeated claim is that the film's opening hacking scene displays a real Nmap scan on screen — this is not accurate for the 1999 original. That verifiable moment, involving Trinity running an authentic Nmap scan against SSH servers on a power-grid system, actually occurs in the sequel The Matrix Reloaded (2003), and has been specifically cited by security researchers and the Nmap project itself as an unusually realistic depiction of hacking for a Hollywood film.

Influences and Legacy

The Matrix draws heavily on cyberpunk literary traditions; author William Gibson called it "arguably the ultimate 'cyberpunk' artifact." The film's technical-aesthetic legacy has been outsized: its "digital rain" effect became a shorthand visual symbol for hacking and computer systems across subsequent film, television, and software culture. The Matrix remains a foundational reference point whenever hacking, digital identity, or simulated reality are depicted in fiction.

Characters

  • Thomas A. Anderson (“Neo”) — played by Keanu Reeves
    A programmer who moonlights as the hacker 'Neo,' selling illegal hacking programs; revealed to be the prophesied 'One' capable of manipulating the Matrix from within.
  • Trinity — played by Carrie-Anne Moss
    A hacker who gained underground fame for breaching a heavily secured IRS database; serves as first mate on Morpheus's ship. (Her widely cited real-Nmap hacking scene occurs in the sequel The Matrix Reloaded, not this film.)
  • Morpheus — played by Laurence Fishburne
    Captain of the hovercraft Nebuchadnezzar and leader of a rebel crew; he frees Neo from the simulation and mentors him.
  • Agent Smith — played by Hugo Weaving
    A sentient security program of the Matrix system tasked with hunting down hackers and rebels; the film's principal antagonist.
