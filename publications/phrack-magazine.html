Phrack Magazine: The Hacker's Ezine

The legacy of Phrack, one of the longest-running and most influential hacker ezines, known for its deep technical articles and philosophical essays.

First released on November 17, 1985, Phrack (a portmanteau of 'phreak' and 'hack') is an electronic magazine, or 'ezine,' that has been a central fixture of the hacker underground for decades. It was founded by two hackers known as 'Taran King' and 'Knight Lightning'.

Phrack set itself apart with its high-quality, deeply technical articles covering everything from telephony and network protocols to exploit development and cryptography. It became a prestigious venue for hackers to publish their research. Seminal texts, such as 'Smashing The Stack For Fun And Profit' by Aleph One and 'The Conscience of a Hacker' by The Mentor, were first published in Phrack.

The magazine was also at the center of a major legal battle in the early 1990s. The U.S. Secret Service raided the home of a Phrack editor for publishing a document about the E911 system, which they claimed was a dangerous trade secret. The case was ultimately dropped when it was revealed the document was publicly available for a small fee. This event, known as the 'E911 Zine' case, became a landmark moment for electronic free speech. Phrack continues to be published sporadically, maintaining its legendary status in the hacker community.
